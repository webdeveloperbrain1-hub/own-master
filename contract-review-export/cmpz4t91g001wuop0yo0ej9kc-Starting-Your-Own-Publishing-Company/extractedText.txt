5.) If you are using a fictitious name, you must file a d/b/a (doing business as) in the
city or county you live. With this, you can get an EIN (employer identification
number) from the IRS. This is important, because if you do start making royalty
income and are paid by the performance rights organizations, you will need to
open a bank account in the name of the publishing company. Otherwise, you
won’t be able to cash the checks issued to you.
6.) If you’re not getting the amount of exposure you need to truly exploit the
potential of your music from your own publishing company, consider finding an
established publishing company and give up 50% of your music for the deal. A
third option is to find a publishing company that will “co-publish” your music
alongside your own existing publishing company.
7.) If you’re wildly successful in publishing your own music and simply can’t keepup with the administration of all incoming royalties, consider hiring an
